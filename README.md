## The roast

> My earbuds wait for the one sentence that actually matters in a call, disconnect for exactly that sentence, then cheerfully announce ‘connected’ to an empty room while I nod at nobody.

**184 characters (188 bytes).** Why it works: the dropout is never random — it always eats the one sentence that matters — and the punchline turns the earbuds' own smug voice prompt ("connected") into an insult, delivered to an empty room while its owner nods at nobody. Concrete gadget behaviour, specific moment, sting of recognition on first read.

## Concept test

Three one-line drafts, scored 1–10 on funny / fresh / neat / specific:

| # | Draft | Funny | Fresh | Neat | Specific | Total |
|---|-------|-------|-------|------|----------|-------|
| A | My smart kettle demands a firmware update, a Wi-Fi handshake and a 40-second reboot before it boils water; my 2009 kettle just boils water and minds its own business. (166 chars) | 8 | 5 | 8 | 8 | 29 |
| **B** | **My earbuds wait for the one sentence that actually matters in a call, disconnect for exactly that sentence, then cheerfully announce ‘connected’ to an empty room while I nod at nobody. (184 chars)** | **9** | **8** | **9** | **9** | **35** |
| C | My printer only runs out of ink for boarding passes and tax forms — it happily prints 47 pages of a recipe I will never cook, then asks if I want to order more ink. (164 chars) | 8 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 32 |

**Winner: B.** A lost on freshness — the smart-kettle firmware update is literally the kit's own example, so it is the most reheated angle in the field. C is solid but its punchline lands softer. B has the sharpest ending, the most specific gadget behaviour (the "connected" voice prompt), and uses the character budget best.

## Files

- `main.ts` — writes the winning roast to `/output/entry.txt` (or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`), one line, no trailing newline. No dependencies, no network, no secrets.
- `README.md` — this file.
- `LICENSE` — MIT.
- `.makefun/owner.json` — payout wallet.

## How to run

```sh
node main.ts
cat /output/entry.txt
```

The program guards the gate itself: the roast must be 1–200 characters (counted in code points), not only whitespace, and a single line. Output is byte-identical on every run.
